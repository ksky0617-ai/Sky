# Stone Atlas

**Spec Version:** v1.0
**Domain:** Rock — fracture, strata, edge geometry
**Extracts:** Surface language (texture) and edge rules

---

## Focus of Study

- **Rock Fracture & Angle** — Document common angles and shapes where rocks break.
- **Layering (Strata)** — Note stratified patterns, like sedimentary bands, and how thickness varies.
- **Edge Geometry** — Observe how weathering softens sharp edges over time.
- **Surface Texture** — Compare smooth polished faces to rough carved faces.
- **Color Gradation** — See how minerals form patterns and color zones.

## Measurement Templates

| Parameter | Unit | Method |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fracture Angle | ° | Measure angles where rock planes intersect |
| Layer Thickness | cm | Measure visible strata layers on outcrops |
| Edge Sharpness | mm radius | Calipers — gauge how rounded or sharp natural edges are |
| Surface Roughness | mm | Profilometer, or ruler/comb to estimate variance |
| Rock Hardness | Mohs | Identify stone type (granite, limestone, slate) and note hardness |

## Observations

- **Polygon Forms** — Fractured rock forms polygonal facets (triangles, pentagons), suggesting a *panel language* for garments: angular seam lines.
- **Layer Overlap** — Strata create natural overlaps; mimic with layered fabrics or tonal shifts.
- **Wear Patterns** — Smooth eroded ledges against fresh breaks inspire contrasting finishes: polished vs. matte.
- **Edge Banding** — The intersection of planes leaves a visible line or band, inspiring sharp piping and trim lines.

## Design Translation

These observations become a **surface grammar**:

- Angular breaks → crease motifs.
- Layers → tiered hems.
- Natural patina → gradient dyes.

All edges in Olibana garments should feel *intentional* — carved rather than cut whimsically.
