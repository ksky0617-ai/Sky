# Light Atlas

**Spec Version:** v1.0
**Domain:** Illumination — time, season, shadow, atmosphere
**Extracts:** Lighting rules, contrast systems, color relationships

---

## Focus of Study

- **Time of Day** — Study light at dawn, midday, golden hour, and the blue hour.
- **Seasonal Sun** — Observe how color temperature shifts between summer and winter, and between overcast and clear skies.
- **Shadows** — Note shadow hardness and softness at different times, in shade vs. direct sun.
- **Atmospheric Effects** — Observe mist, haze, and rain on light diffusion and color.

## Observations

- **Hue Palette** — Sunrise yields warm pinks and oranges; midday yields bright blue-white; dusk yields deep gold and purple. These define seasonal and accent colors.
- **Soft vs. Hard Light** — At golden hour shadows are long and colors rich, suggesting deep textures and warm tones. Harsh noon light casts crisp shadows, suggesting monochrome contrast.
- **Reflectivity** — Fabrics catch light differently: silk gleams, wool absorbs. Materials are chosen for the appropriate reflectance.
- **Shadow Pattern** — Tree canopies cast dappled shade; the same mottling can drive prints or layering.

## Design Translation

The Light Atlas governs **color and material choices**:

- A fabric's sheen is selected to match a documented natural reflectivity.
- Shoot lighting is planned to reproduce a specific time of day's mood.
- Codified lighting rules follow: higher-contrast palettes for midday looks, softer gradients for evening collections.
