# Forest Atlas

**Spec Version:** v1.0
**Domain:** Trees — branching, bark, canopy
**Extracts:** Hierarchical ratios, repeatable patterns, growth-inspired proportion systems

---

## Focus of Study

- **Branching Structure** — Study trunk-to-branch ratios, branching angles, and symmetry vs. randomness.
- **Bark Texture** — Note the scales and patterns in bark: horizontal rings, deep furrows, mosaic plates.
- **Canopy Density** — Observe leaf distribution in sunlight vs. shade.
- **Species Variation** — Compare structures across species (conifer vs. maple vs. cedar).

## Measurement Templates

| Parameter | Unit | Method |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Branch Angle | ° | Measure typical angles between branch and trunk |
| Trunk-to-Canopy Ratio | ratio | Canopy width vs. trunk diameter |
| Leaf Cluster Spacing | cm | Average distance between leaf clusters or needle groups |
| Bark Ridge Height | mm | For textured barks (cypress, cedar), measure ridge frequency and height |

## Observations

- **Hierarchy** — Trees form a natural hierarchy (trunk → branches → twigs). Designs use nested proportions: main seams → sub-seams → stitching.
- **Repetition** — Repeating motifs (leaf shapes, needle clusters) abstract cleanly into prints and knits.
- **Organic Curve** — Branches grow in flowing curves; pant and sleeve seams echo these arcs.
- **Textural Contrast** — Smooth trunk against rough foliage suggests combining smooth panels with textured knits or embroidery.

## Design Translation

From the Forest Atlas we derive *proportions and patterns*:

- A shawl mimics the silhouette of a canopy.
- A knit pattern abstracts a leaf vein structure.
- The branching ratio informs how widths change: shoulder to hem, body to collar.
